The Arsenal is the part of riff.quest that has nothing to do with your fingers. You spend **Fame** — never points — on cases full of virtual guitars and pedals, keep the ones you like, and equip them to build a rig. None of it makes you a better guitarist. That's rather the point: it's the reward for the work you did elsewhere.

## Rarity and item level

Every item has a rarity, which sets what it's worth and how far it can be pushed.

<TierScale items="Common|Uncommon|Rare|Epic|Legendary|Mythic" caption="Rarity isn't the whole story — condition, vintage and where the instrument was made all feed into its Item Level, so two Rares are rarely equal." />

## The Workshop

Scrapping gear you don't want breaks it down into **parts**, and parts are what the Workshop runs on. There are two jobs on the bench.

<StepList steps="Restore::Raises an item's condition one grade at a time, up to Museum. It costs more the better the condition and the rarer the item, and it adds Item Level — but it never raises what the game will buy the item back for. Restoring is for keeps, not for flipping|Build::Fits one more upgrade. There's no ceiling on build levels, but every level asks for more than the last" />

<StatRow stats="6 parts:Build 1|90 parts:Build 10|401 parts:Build 15|1797 parts:Build 20" caption="The cost climbs steeply while the gain per level stays flat — deep builds are a long-term project, not an evening's work." />

Higher builds also get pickier: they want more *different* kinds of parts, they refuse anything below a minimum rarity, they ask for rare Unique parts near the top, and they require the item to be in good condition first — eventually Museum condition. Each build level adds a flat amount of Item Level depending on rarity, and the cost is the same whatever the rarity, so parts poured into a Mythic go a lot further than the same parts spent on a Common.

<BlogAlert type="tip">
Before you scrap anything, check the Rig tab. Parts are easy to come by; a specific instrument rolled with the condition and features you like isn't.
</BlogAlert>

## The Trader

The Market tab has two halves: listings from other players, and the Trader — the game's own shop, and the only place parts can be bought at all. It restocks once a day and everyone sees the same stock.

Parts are sold by the piece, each with its own daily limit, so a single day's Fame is always enough to buy *something*, and one part offer each day is discounted by 30%. The parts the drops are stingy with turn up here more often — and cost more.

<StatRow stats="2 Fame:a screw|12 Fame:a standard part|35 Fame:an epic part|450 Fame:a legendary part" caption="Legendary parts are priced to rescue a build the drops have stalled, not to supply one. Unique parts are never sold." />

The Trader also stocks **two guitars and two pedals a day**, each a specific instrument whose condition and features you can see before you pay. They're expensive on purpose — buying one and selling it straight back always loses Fame, and a Legendary is a month of saving. The top rarities only appear every week or two.

## Rig Level

Your **Rig Level** is the sum of the Item Level of everything currently equipped in your Rig — guitars plus pedalboard effects. It's the number the [gear board](/wiki/leaderboard-and-seasons) ranks you on. Only equipped gear counts, so swapping in something stronger moves it immediately, and Workshop work only pays off if it went into something you actually use.

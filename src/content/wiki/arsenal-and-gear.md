Arsenal (`/arsenal`) is riff.quest's gear-collecting meta-game — guitars and pedals you unlock by spending **Fame**, not points. It has six tabs: Cases, Collection, Workshop, Dex, Rig, and Market.

## Opening cases

<StatRow stats="150 Fame:Standard Case|300 Fame:Premium Guitar / Effect Case|400 Fame:Elite Guitar / Effect Case (Rare+ only)|200 Fame:Featured Case (rotates every 3 days)" caption="The Standard Case can drop any rarity, weighted heavily toward Common; Elite cases guarantee Rare or better." />

The Featured Case rotates its pool of items every 3 days on a fixed schedule — everyone sees the same rotation.

## Rarity & item level

Every item has a rarity tier — Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic — which sets its base sell value and a level bonus. On top of that, an item's actual **Item Level** also factors in its condition (Relic through Museum), vintage, and country-of-origin prestige, so two items of the same rarity aren't necessarily equal.

## The Workshop

Scrapping a guitar or pedal tears it down into **parts** — the raw material of the Workshop, where you turn a wide collection into a deep one. There are two jobs on the bench.

**Restore** raises an item's condition one grade at a time, up to Museum. It costs any parts from the matching half of your wallet, gets steeper the higher the grade and the rarer the item, and adds Item Level. What it deliberately does *not* do is raise the price the game buys the item back for — a restored instrument keeps the collector value it was minted with, so restoring is an investment in your Rig, not a way to flip Fame.

**Build** fits one more mod. There is **no build cap** — instead every level demands more:

<StatRow stats="Build 1:6 parts|Build 10:90 parts|Build 15:401 parts|Build 20:1797 parts" caption="The price curve is geometric while the payoff per level is flat, so the wall is one you feel rather than one that stops you." />

Requirements widen on four axes at once. The price above, but also *how many different* parts a job needs (from Build 13 you need one of every part the item is physically built from), the *minimum tier* accepted (Epic from Build 8, Legendary from Build 19), and **Unique parts** — from Build 19 you need one, then another every seven levels. Every level also costs a little Fame. On top of that, the item's condition gates the whole thing: Good to start building, Mint from Build 8, Museum from Build 19.

Each build level is worth a flat amount of Item Level depending on rarity — Common and Uncommon +1, Rare and Epic +2, Legendary +3, Mythic +4. The *cost* is identical for every rarity, which is the whole point: parts poured into a Mythic go four times as far as parts poured into a Common, so choosing what goes on the bench matters.

## Rig Level

Your **Rig Level** — the number that ranks you on the [gear leaderboard](/wiki/leaderboard-and-seasons) — is simply the sum of the Item Level of everything you currently have equipped in your Rig: guitars plus pedalboard effects. Swapping in a higher-level item raises it immediately; it's not tied to your whole collection, only what's equipped right now. Workshop restores and build levels feed straight into it, as long as the item they went into is the one you have equipped.

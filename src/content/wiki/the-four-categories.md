Every minute you log goes into one of four buckets. You meet them the moment you save your first session, and they follow you through the whole app: your statistics, your skills, your daily quests and your practice history are all sliced the same four ways.

They are worth **exactly the same** in points. Twenty minutes of theory pays what twenty minutes of shredding pays. The split isn't a scoring rule, it's a mirror.

## What goes where

<Screenshot src="/images/wiki/four-categories-log.webp" alt="The four category cards on the log form, side by side: Technique, Theory, Hearing and Creative work, each with its own icon, a question-mark button and a pair of hours and minutes wheels" caption="The same four cards, every time you log. Each one has its own clock, so a session is split as you enter it rather than assigned to a single category afterwards." />

The app shows examples of what belongs in each behind the small **?** on those cards. They're deliberately broad, because most real practice doesn't sort itself neatly.

<Checklist items="Technique::Playing and learning songs, warming up, practising rhythm, and the physical techniques: alternate picking, legato, bending, vibrato, tapping|Theory::Transcribing and analysing songs, learning the notes on the fretboard, reading music, chords, scales and harmony|Hearing::Playing or listening to songs by ear, identifying intervals and chords, singing, recognising rhythms|Creative work::Writing your own songs, improvising, composing, and working on your own sound" />

<BlogAlert type="tip">
Don't agonise over the edges. Learning a solo by ear is Hearing while you're working it out and Technique once you're drilling it, and either answer is fine. The only wrong move is putting everything in Technique out of habit.
</BlogAlert>

## Why they exist

If points were all that mattered, one bucket would do. Four exist because they're the only thing in the app that can tell you what you're *avoiding*.

<StepList steps="They show you the gap::Your Progress screen splits your history four ways. Three weeks with a flat Hearing bar is information you will not get any other way|They feed different skills::Each of the 23 tracked skills belongs to one category, so a category you never touch is a whole group of skills sitting still|They drive quests::Several daily tasks ask for a specific category, or for two or three in one day, which is the app nudging you off your favourite" />

## How the skills divide up

Every tracked skill sits under exactly one category. The count alone tells you something about the instrument:

<Checklist items="Technique — 11 skills::Alternate picking, finger independence, legato, sweep picking, rhythm, bending, tapping, articulation, vibrato, hybrid picking, string skipping|Theory — 4 skills::Chords, scales, harmony, music theory|Hearing — 4 skills::Ear training, harmonic ear, transcription, rhythm recognition|Creative work — 4 skills::Phrasing, improvisation, composition, audio production" />

<BlogAlert type="info">
Technique having half the skills doesn't make it worth more. It reflects how finely guitar technique gets subdivided, not how much it counts.
</BlogAlert>

## Who fills them in for you

<Checklist items="Guided practice::Routines, Auto Plan, Learning Path and roadmaps tag the categories themselves, because the exercise already knows what it trains|Exercises::Each one carries its own category, and some are tagged as mixed when they genuinely train more than one thing|Songs::Practising a song splits its time between Technique and Hearing, since that's what learning a song actually is|Free Timer::A separate stopwatch per category, so the split is decided while you play rather than afterwards|Manual Log::Entirely yours to fill in, and the only place the split can drift from the truth" />

## Getting an honest split

<StepList steps="Tag while it's fresh::A session logged three days later gets rounded into whatever you usually pick|Don't split hairs::Five-minute precision is not the point. Being roughly right every day beats being exactly right once|Let the neglected one pull you::If a category has been flat for a month, that's your next session choosing itself" />

<BlogAlert type="takeaway">
The categories cost you nothing and pay you nothing. Their entire job is to make one uncomfortable fact visible: almost everybody practises the thing they're already good at.
</BlogAlert>

Songs are the part of riff.quest that looks least like homework. You keep a list of what you're learning, drag songs along as you get them under your fingers, and the app quietly turns that into points and a difficulty rating for your playing.

## What mastering a song pays

Moving a song to **Mastered** pays **+30 points**, as long as you've logged at least **30 minutes** of practice on that specific song first. Mark it earlier and the status changes without paying anything. Move it back out again and the +30 is taken back off. These points go on your lifetime total and your level; they stay out of the monthly season, which is there to measure the month's practice.

<BlogAlert type="tip">
Practise the song from its own page, because that's what builds up the 10 minutes, and it's also what tells the app which songs you actually work on.
</BlogAlert>

## Difficulty ratings and tiers

Anyone can rate a song from 1 to 10. Your first rating on a song pays **+3 Fame** (rating again is possible after a short cooldown, but only the first one pays). Everyone's ratings are averaged into the song's tier:

A song nobody has rated yet shows a **?** instead of a tier.

## Skill Power

Skill Power is the app's one-number answer to "how hard is the stuff this person can actually play?" It's built from your **10 hardest mastered songs**, weighted so the hardest counts most, with a small bonus for having mastered a lot of songs overall.

<Screenshot src="/images/wiki/skill-power.webp" alt="The Skill Power summary: 24 mastered, 29 total, 4h 6m time spent, a large A badge labelled Current tier, and a note reading '3 more songs as hard as S-Tier to level up'" caption="Your current tier sits on the right, and the line underneath tells you exactly what it would take to move it: not more songs, but harder ones." />

It's capped at the tier of your single hardest song, so a hundred easy songs can never out-rank one genuinely difficult one. That's why the app asks for songs *as hard as* the next tier rather than just more of them. You need at least **5 mastered songs** before it appears at all.

## Playlists

<Checklist items="Playlist::A free-form collection: a set list, a genre, songs for one technique|Path::An ordered journey through songs, meant to be worked through in sequence|Top::A ranked list, capped at 10 songs" />

Public playlists show up in Discover, ranked by how popular they are, where someone saving a playlist counts three times as much as a like. Sharing pays: when someone saves your public playlist you get **+20 Fame**, and a like pays **+5 Fame**.

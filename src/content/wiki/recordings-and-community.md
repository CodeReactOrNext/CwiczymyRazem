Recording yourself is the single most useful practice habit there is, and also the one nobody enjoys. riff.quest gives it somewhere to go: a shared wall of playing, where the standard is honest rather than perfect.

<ClickPath steps="Recordings" caption="Everything the community has posted, plus a tab with only your own." />

## Posting a recording

<StepList steps="Record::Phone, webcam or interface, whatever you have. Nobody is marking your camera work|Upload it to YouTube::Recordings are shared as video links rather than files|Add it here::Paste the link, give it a title, and optionally connect it to the song you were playing|Share it::It appears on the recordings wall, where other players can watch, like and comment" />

Tying a recording to a song makes it show up on that song's page too, which is how you end up watching five other people's takes on the riff you're stuck on.

## Why bother

<Checklist items="It's the fastest feedback you'll get::A camera hears the timing problems you don't|Recording is a healthy habit::Tick it when you log the session and it's worth a point|It's how challenges work::Monthly challenge entries are recordings, and those pay 100 points and 300 Fame each|Comments::People respond to recordings far more often than to numbers on a leaderboard" />

## Other ways the community pays

<StatRow stats="+300 Fame:a challenge recording|+20 Fame:someone saves your playlist|+5 Fame:someone likes your playlist|+5 Fame:a thank-you for an exercise you shared" caption="Plus +1 Fame the first time another player completes an exercise you wrote, and +3 Fame the first time you rate a song's difficulty." />

<BlogAlert type="tip">
If you want a steady Fame income without grinding, share what you already make: publish the practice exercises you write and the playlists you build. They keep paying long after you've forgotten about them.
</BlogAlert>

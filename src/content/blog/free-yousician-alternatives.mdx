Yousician bundles four things into one subscription: a video-led guitar course, microphone feedback on what you play, a catalogue of licensed songs and a streak that gets you back tomorrow. No single free tool replaces all four, but every one of those jobs has a free stand-in. JustinGuitar's website course and Andy Guitar's Beginners Course cover the lessons, with no card and no daily cap. Songsterr, Ultimate Guitar and Chordify cover the songs, each with its own limits on playback or daily plays. Riff Quest covers feedback on the notes you play in its exercises and, more importantly, the planning, logging and quests that keep a practice habit alive. Before switching anything, read the free-tier section: Yousician's own terms allow some access without paying, so the question is which job you are actually paying for.

**Disclosure: Riff Quest publishes this guide and is included in the comparison.**
:::

**Short version: use JustinGuitar for the course, Riff Quest for the routine, the feedback and the log, and Songsterr or Ultimate Guitar for the songs.** Andy Guitar is the second free course worth knowing, and Chordify is the quickest way to get chords for a song neither tab site has.

This guide is about replacing a lesson app. If the part of Yousician you will miss is the scored play-along with a real guitar — the Rocksmith side of it — our [free Rocksmith alternatives comparison](/blog/free-rocksmith-alternatives) covers StringTheory, fee[dB]ack, Soundlane and TuxGuitar with a screenshot of each, and those tools are not repeated here. Features and access information were checked on **16 September 2026**.

## Is Yousician Free?

Yousician's terms of service say you "may access parts of the Services for free", while other services and products require payment. The official terms do not specify a daily allowance or list which guitar lessons stay free permanently. Third-party reviews in 2026 describe a cap of roughly 15 minutes of play per day on a free account; that figure comes from reviewers, not from Yousician's documentation, so check what your own account unlocks before planning around it. [Yousician terms of service](https://yousician.com/terms-of-service)

The advertised **Premium+ trial lasts seven days**. Yousician's support pages explain that starting the trial requires a payment method, and the terms state that a paid subscription begins automatically unless you cancel before the trial ends. A trial is a way to evaluate the app, not an ongoing free practice plan. [Trial conditions](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000336137-How-to-start-a-free-premium-trial)

For continued paid access, Yousician separates Premium from Premium+. Both include unlimited lessons; Premium covers one instrument, while Premium+ adds all five instruments and the full popular-song catalogue. A family membership supports up to four accounts. Yousician prices its plans by country and does not publish a public rate card; figures reported by third parties in 2026 range from roughly $15 to $30 per month. [Membership comparison](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005189525-Premium-membership-options-in-Yousician)

I set a calendar reminder on the day I start any trial that asks for a card, after one converted on me years ago and charged a full year before I noticed. A seven-day trial is fine to use; just decide on day one which day you will cancel.

## Which Part of Yousician Are You Replacing?

Yousician is easier to replace once you name the job you would miss. Most people who leave miss one or two of the four, not all of them, and the free tool that fits depends on which.

| Job | What Yousician gives you | Free stand-in |
|---|---|---|
| **Structured lessons** | An educator-designed guitar path with videos, from holding the guitar onwards | JustinGuitar's Beginner Course, Andy Guitar's Beginners Course |
| **Feedback from your guitar** | Microphone scoring of accuracy and timing inside lessons | Riff Quest's Pitch Detect in supported exercises; the note-highway games in the Rocksmith comparison |
| **Songs to play** | Licensed interactive arrangements, mostly on Premium+ | Songsterr and Ultimate Guitar tabs, Chordify chord charts |
| **A reason to come back** | Scores, levels, badges and a daily streak | Riff Quest's quests, levels, rankings and session history |

The one thing no free tool reproduces is the combination: Yousician's lessons, feedback and songs sit in a single flow with one score. Replacing it usually means one app for the teaching and one for the practising, which is a workable setup rather than a compromise for most players.

## For the Lessons: JustinGuitar and Andy Guitar

### JustinGuitar — The Free Course Most People Should Start With

JustinGuitar's Beginner Course is a structured route through guitar basics, open chords, rhythm, chord changes and songs, organised into grades and modules on the website. The Grade 1 course is free on the site, with no daily cap and no card. The separately offered Lessons & Songs app and the premium products are not the same thing as the free website course, so start on the website if free is the point. [Beginner Course, Grade 1](https://www.justinguitar.com/classes/beginner-guitar-course-grade-one) · [Free courses and separate products](https://www.justinguitar.com/store/apps)

JustinGuitar replaces Yousician's teaching, not its feedback: the website course tells you what to play and shows you how, and then it is your ears that judge the result. The built-in song player and My Practice Assistant give it more structure than a plain playlist of videos, but nothing in the free course listens to your guitar.

My own first chords came from video lessons, and the feature that mattered most was the pause button — I could rewind a chord change fifteen times without a teacher losing patience. Yousician's scoring is nice to have, but a clear video and a slow, honest repetition of the movement is what actually taught my hands.

### Andy Guitar — A Second Free Beginner Route

Andy Guitar's Beginners Course runs from Level 0 to Level 9, starting with tuning, chord diagrams, reading tab and strumming, and moving through chords, songs, fingerstyle and minor keys. The site describes lots of free content, a free account that tracks and saves your progress, and a paid Access All Areas membership that funds the site and unlocks the rest. Andy Guitar also covers ukulele, which JustinGuitar's free guitar course does not. [Beginners Course](https://www.andyguitar.co.uk/online-lessons/courses/beginner-course) · [Courses and membership](https://www.andyguitar.co.uk/courses)

Andy Guitar suits a player who wants a song-first, acoustic-leaning course, and the ten-day acoustic starter course is a low-commitment way to find out. JustinGuitar's course is the deeper of the two on theory and rhythm; either one covers the Yousician lesson path for a first year of playing.

## For the Songs: Songsterr, Ultimate Guitar and Chordify

None of the free song tools gives you what Yousician's Premium+ catalogue gives: a licensed arrangement that scrolls, plays and scores you. What they give is the written material — tabs or chord charts for the song — which you then play against the original recording or a metronome.

### Songsterr — Accurate Tabs You Can Read for Free

Songsterr keeps a smaller catalogue than Ultimate Guitar with tighter quality control, and its tabs render as playable notation with the song's actual rhythm. Browsing and reading tabs is free. Songsterr Plus adds the tools that make song practice efficient — pause-free sync with the original audio, speed changes without pitch changes, looping, solo and mute per instrument, and downloads — and the Plus page does not publish a price. [Songsterr Plus](https://www.songsterr.com/a/wa/plus)

### Ultimate Guitar — The Largest Free Catalogue of Tabs and Chords

Ultimate Guitar hosts community tabs and chord charts for essentially every popular song, usually in several versions at different difficulty levels, and reading them is free. Ultimate Guitar Pro is the paid tier that unlocks playback and offline saving. Community submissions vary in accuracy, so for a well-known song pick the top-rated version and cross-check against Songsterr if the rhythm looks doubtful. Songsterr's rhythm notation saved me more than once after an Ultimate Guitar chord sheet had me strumming a verse in the wrong place for a week. The chord sheet was right about the chords; it just had no way of telling me when they changed.

### Chordify — Chords for Songs No One Has Tabbed

Chordify works out the chords of a recording automatically and displays them in time with the audio, which makes it the quickest route to a chord chart for an obscure song. Chordify's support centre describes a free allowance of a couple of songs per day, doubled with a free account, with advertisements, and puts transposing, capo hints, looping and uploads on the paid Premium tiers. Treat the daily figure as something to confirm on the site, since it can change. [Is Chordify free?](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/articles/360001945038-Is-Chordify-free)

Chordify's automatic detection is a starting point rather than a transcription: on a song with a busy arrangement it can simplify or misplace a chord, so listen back and correct the chart by ear.

## For Feedback From Your Guitar: Riff Quest Pitch Detect

Riff Quest's Pitch Detect listens through a microphone or an audio interface in supported exercises and shows whether the notes you play match the tablature. Pitch Detect checks pitch, not tone, dynamics or the musical quality of a whole performance — [note detection](/wiki/note-detection) explains those limits. It runs in the browser without an account upgrade, a subscription or a payment card.

Feedback in Riff Quest is tied to exercises rather than to a song catalogue: a pentatonic box, a chord-change drill or a picking pattern, played against colour-coded tablature with a tempo control and bar selection for loops. Progress points come from the practice you record, not from the hit percentage alone, so a rough attempt at a slow tempo still counts as work done.

For a scored, scrolling note highway with a full song loaded, the tools in the [free Rocksmith alternatives comparison](/blog/free-rocksmith-alternatives) are the better match: StringTheory and fee[dB]ack on the desktop, Soundlane in the browser. Those games take Guitar Pro files rather than shipping licensed songs, so pair them with a tab from Songsterr or Ultimate Guitar.

## For the Streak: Riff Quest Quests, Levels and Session History

Riff Quest's motivation layer is the part of Yousician that most free courses leave out. Daily quests, levels, skills and rankings sit on top of a practice log, so the thing that gets rewarded is the practice you did, whether it happened in a Riff Quest exercise, a JustinGuitar lesson or on a song from Songsterr.

Free Timer records a session while you play it, and Manual Log covers a session you finished away from the screen, up to seven days after the fact. That is how a JustinGuitar lesson or twenty minutes on a song ends up in the same history as an exercise. [Practice modes](/wiki/practice-modes-overview)

For the "what should I practise today?" part of Yousician's path, [Auto Plan](/wiki/exercise-plans-and-auto-plan) builds a session around the length you have, with category and difficulty filters, and ready-made routines cover the same need with a fixed list. The [Learning Path](/wiki/journey-and-scale-tree) adds guided modules — currently Fundamentals and Fretboard Mastery — and Scale Map offers another route through scale shapes, though neither is a video-led curriculum in Yousician's sense.

Guitar Pro Files lets you practise imported tabs with playback, and song tracking records time against a specific piece of repertoire. Riff Quest ships an exercise library rather than a song catalogue, so songs are still material you bring yourself.

I built the quests in Riff Quest because a bare practice log did nothing for me on the days I did not feel like playing. A quest that asks for ten minutes of technique is easy to say yes to, and ten minutes is usually enough to turn into thirty once the guitar is in my hands.

## Which Free Yousician Alternative Should You Pick?

- **You want someone to teach you, in order:** JustinGuitar's Beginner Course, with Andy Guitar as the acoustic, song-first second option.
- **You want to hear whether you played the right notes:** Riff Quest's Pitch Detect for exercises; a note-highway game from the Rocksmith comparison for whole songs.
- **You want the songs:** Songsterr for accuracy, Ultimate Guitar for breadth, Chordify for a chord chart nobody has written.
- **You want the streak:** Riff Quest's quests, levels and session history — free, no card.
- **You want all four in one app and can pay for it:** stay on Yousician and compare Premium against Premium+ before choosing.

For a beginner with no subscription budget, the combination that works is **one free lesson source plus one place to plan and record practice**. Watch a lesson, apply it for a few minutes, then log what improved and what needs another attempt. JustinGuitar or Andy Guitar covers the explanation and Riff Quest the routine and the log; that is a suggested way of working, not an integration between the two.

## FAQs

### Is there a free version of Yousician?

Yousician's terms allow access to parts of the service without paying, but the official documentation does not state a daily allowance or list the lessons that stay free. Reviewers in 2026 describe a cap of around 15 minutes of play per day on a free account. Check what your own account unlocks, and treat the seven-day Premium+ trial as a paid subscription that starts later rather than as free access.

### Which free app is most like Yousician?

No free app matches the whole package. JustinGuitar's website course is the closest match for the lessons, and it is free without a cap. For the scored play-along part, Soundlane runs in the browser with a microphone and takes Guitar Pro files, and is covered in the Rocksmith comparison. For the streak, quests and practice history, Riff Quest is the closest free match.

### Will I get Yousician's song library in a free alternative?

Don't assume so. Importing your own tab, tracking a song you are learning and receiving a licensed interactive arrangement are three different features. Songsterr, Ultimate Guitar and Chordify give you the written material for a song; playing it in time with feedback is a separate step in a separate tool.

### Can I get feedback from my guitar without paying?

Yes, for notes. Riff Quest's Pitch Detect checks whether the notes you play match the exercise, in the browser, without a subscription. The note-highway games in the Rocksmith comparison score whole songs from Guitar Pro files. None of the free options judges tone, dynamics or overall musicality, and this guide did not run a controlled accuracy test on any of them.

### Can I practise without enabling a microphone?

In Riff Quest, ordinary tablature practice runs without detection; some assessed activities require it. JustinGuitar and Andy Guitar never listen to your guitar, so with no microphone you judge the result by ear, ideally with a phone recording to play back. [Detection requirements](/wiki/note-detection)

### Does this guide cover piano, bass, ukulele or singing?

No — the comparison is guitar-only. Yousician's Premium+ plan covers five instruments, which is a real advantage if several people in a household play different things. Among the free tools, Andy Guitar publishes ukulele lessons and Riff Quest is guitar-focused; check each site for the other instruments.

### Is the seven-day trial a safe way to test Yousician?

The seven-day trial is a reasonable way to see the product, as long as you treat it as a paid subscription with a delayed start. The trial needs a payment method up front and converts unless you cancel before the seven days are up, so set a reminder on the day you start it.

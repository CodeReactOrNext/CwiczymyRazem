If you want to keep learning guitar without a subscription, start by deciding which part of Yousician you want to replace. Video instruction, feedback from your guitar, arrangements of popular songs and a reason to practise tomorrow are four different needs. One free tool may cover several of them; it may take two to cover your routine.

:::tip
**Disclosure: Riff Quest publishes this guide and is included in the comparison.**
:::

**For free interactive exercises and practice tracking, try Riff Quest. For a beginner video course, start with JustinGuitar's website. For playing your own tabs with note detection, look at Soundlane, StringTheory or fee[dB]ack.** TuxGuitar is another option if reading and editing tabs matters more to you than automatic scoring.

This guide compares Yousician with Riff Quest first, then lists the other alternatives below. Features and access information were checked on **16 September 2026**.

## Is Yousician Actually Free?

There are two different questions here: can you access anything without paying, and can you keep using the full learning experience without a subscription?

Yousician's terms allow access to parts of the service for free. They do not specify a universal daily allowance or a complete list of permanently free guitar lessons. That is why this guide does not promise "10 minutes free every day" or "15 minutes free every day" the way other comparisons do — check what your own account actually unlocks. [Yousician terms of service, Payment Policy](https://yousician.com/terms-of-service)

The advertised **Premium+ trial lasts seven days**. Yousician's support pages explain that starting a trial requires a payment method, and that it turns into a paid subscription unless you cancel before it ends. A trial can help you evaluate the app, but it is not an ongoing free practice plan. [Trial conditions](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000336137-How-to-start-a-free-premium-trial)

For continued paid access, Yousician separates Premium from Premium+. Both include unlimited lessons; Premium covers one instrument, while Premium+ adds all five instruments and access to the full popular-song offering. A family membership supports up to four accounts. [Membership comparison](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005189525-Premium-membership-options-in-Yousician)

## What You Can Do for Free in Riff Quest

Riff Quest is useful when the question is "what should I practise today, and how do I keep track of it?" The exercise library, session history and community song difficulty ratings are available without a subscription or a payment card.

### Practise With Interactive Tablature

Open an exercise, choose a manageable tempo and follow the notes. The screen below shows **Pentatonic Box 1 — Up and Down**, with colour-coded strings, fret numbers, bar navigation and playback controls. You can work on a single passage instead of restarting the whole exercise from the top every time.

Pitch Detect listens through a microphone or an audio interface in supported exercises. It is useful for checking that the right notes are coming out, but it is not a verdict on your tone, dynamics or overall musical performance — [note detection](/wiki/note-detection) describes those limits. Progress points come from the practice you record, not from the hit percentage alone.

### Build a Session Around the Time You Have

You can follow a ready-made routine or arrange exercises into your own plan. [Auto Plan](/wiki/exercise-plans-and-auto-plan) builds a session around the length you choose, with category and difficulty filters, and you can adjust the selection before you start. That suits a player who wants a practical session rather than a long browse through a library.

### Keep Your Own Material and Your Practice History

Guitar Pro Files lets you practise imported tabs with playback. Free Timer records practice as you do it, while Manual Log covers sessions you finished away from the screen — so time spent on a song, a lesson or an exercise of your own still lands in the same notebook. [Ways to practise](/wiki/practice-modes-overview)

### Follow a Path When You Need Direction

Riff Quest has guided progression too. The [Learning Path](/wiki/journey-and-scale-tree) currently covers Fundamentals and Fretboard Mastery, with further modules marked as coming soon, and Scale Map offers another route through scale shapes and patterns. That is structure, but it is not an identical replacement for Yousician's video-led curriculum.

## Which Should You Choose?

- **A guided course with integrated song arrangements:** evaluate Yousician and check which plan includes the material you want.
- **Free exercises, your own routine and a record of your practice:** start with Riff Quest.
- **Someone explaining the basics on video without an app subscription:** use JustinGuitar's free website course.
- **A game that listens while you play your own tabs:** compare the play-along tools below.

For a beginner on a zero-subscription budget, a useful combination is **a free video lesson plus a short practice routine**. Watch a lesson, spend a few minutes applying it, then record what improved and what needs another attempt. JustinGuitar can cover the explanation and Riff Quest the routine and the log. That is a suggested way of working, not an automatic integration between the two.

The free Beginner Course is a structured route through guitar basics, chords, rhythm and songs. Start on the website: the separately offered Lessons & Songs app and the premium products are not the same thing as the free course. This is the one to investigate if what you mainly want is clear teaching rather than a note-highway game. ## FAQs

### Will I get Yousician's song library in a free alternative?

Don't assume so. Importing your own tab, tracking a song you are learning and receiving a licensed interactive arrangement are three different features. Check where each tool's material comes from before you switch for one particular song.

### Can I practise without enabling a microphone?

In Riff Quest, ordinary tablature practice runs without detection; some assessed activities require it. With detection off, use your ears and a recording to judge the result. [Detection requirements](/wiki/note-detection)

### Is one app's note detection more accurate than another's?

This comparison does not establish that. No controlled audio test was carried out. Feature lists alone cannot tell you how either tool will respond to your guitar, your input device, your room and your playing.

### What does "free" mean in this guide?

It means the functionality named here could be used without buying a subscription or a licence at the time we checked. It does not mean every product from that company is free, that hardware is included, or that a trial lasts indefinitely.

### Is the seven-day trial a safe way to test Yousician?

It is a reasonable way to see the product, as long as you treat it as a paid subscription that starts later. It needs a payment method up front and converts unless you cancel before the seven days are up, so set a reminder on the day you start it.

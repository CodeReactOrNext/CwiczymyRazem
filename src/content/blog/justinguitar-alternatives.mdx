**For a different teacher, compare Andy Guitar's starter lessons. For a paid guided course, consider Fender Play. For exercises, session planning and a practice log, use Riff Quest. For learning specific songs from tabs, look at Songsterr.** These JustinGuitar alternatives cover different needs; a tab player or practice tracker does not replace a teacher introducing a new skill.

Before switching, check which JustinGuitar product you are using. Its free website lessons, Lessons & Songs app, Tabs subscription and other paid courses are not one interchangeable package. If the problem is an app subscription, the solution might be using the website lessons rather than abandoning Justin's teaching altogether.

## First Check: Website Lessons or the JustinGuitar App?

The [JustinGuitar store](https://www.justinguitar.com/apps) says its beginner course and many other lessons are free, alongside separate paid products. The [Lessons & Songs app page](https://www.justinguitar.com/store/lessons-songs-app) and [Tabs product page](https://www.justinguitar.com/store/tabs) describe different offers. Website access should not be used as evidence that all app content or tab features are included at no cost.

That distinction matters when deciding what to change. If you like the explanations but not the app workflow, try a website lesson with a simple timer and a written exercise list. If the explanations themselves do not click, compare a different teacher on the same topic. If you understand the lesson but cannot play the exercise yet, the missing ingredient may be a smaller practice task rather than another course.

## 1. Andy Guitar: Hear the Skill Explained Another Way

Andy Guitar is worth comparing when the main issue is how a lesson is explained. The official [first starter lesson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BBz-Jyr23M4) is publicly available on Andy's own YouTube channel. His newer [Beginner Course offer](https://shop.andyguitar.co.uk/products/andy-guitar-beginners-course) is a separate paid product: the checked UK page listed £45 for three months of access. Do not confuse that offer with free starter videos or assume that all beginner content has the same access model.

**Choose it if:** a second explanation helps you understand a chord shape, rhythm or physical movement. Watch a beginner lesson at your current level, then pause and try the task. The useful question is whether you know what to do with the guitar afterwards, not whether you enjoyed watching the whole video.

**Its limit:** changing teachers also changes the order in which topics are introduced. A later lesson in a new course may assume skills that your previous course has not covered yet. Spend a little time checking those prerequisites before jumping to the lesson with the most appealing song.

Try one explanation before changing your whole routine. Write down the teacher's exercise, the part you found clearer and the question that remains. If a short explanation solves the immediate confusion, you can use it as a supplement. You do not need to follow two complete beginner courses in parallel to benefit from hearing a second perspective.

## 2. Fender Play: A Guided Subscription Option

Fender's [course overview](https://www.fender.com/play) presents a guided path through songs and guitar skills, alongside Feedback Mode and backing tracks. It is a useful comparison for someone who wants a packaged course experience rather than assembling separate teaching and practice tools.

**Choose it if:** you prefer the way its lessons demonstrate the skills you want to learn and you expect to use the package regularly. Inspect the actual song and skill choices that matter to you. A long list of available material is less useful than finding the next few lessons you would genuinely practise.

**Its limit:** subscription access is an ongoing commitment, and a guided path still requires you to repeat difficult material. The [US subscription page](https://www.fender.com/products/fender-play-subscription) displayed $19.99 monthly and $149.99 annually on 16 October 2026. That annual amount is paid for the year; offers and currencies elsewhere may differ.

## 3. Riff Quest: Keep the Lessons, Change the Practice

Riff Quest fits the player who understands the lesson but needs a way to act on it consistently. It combines an exercise library, practice plans, session records and quests. You can choose a few tasks for a short session rather than deciding again each time you pick up the guitar. Access is free and community-supported, with no subscription required for the exercise catalogue.

**Choose it if:** you keep returning to lessons without spending enough time on the actual movement, or you lose track of what you worked on last week. A useful first plan might contain a warm-up, one exercise addressing the current difficulty and a song section that uses the same skill. The [practice planner](/guitar-practice-planner) explains how to arrange those blocks.

Pitch Detect provides feedback in supported exercises, but it cannot replace your ears or a teacher's judgement. A detected pitch is not proof of good tone, relaxed movement or musical phrasing. The [note-detection guide](/wiki/note-detection) describes what the feature measures and where it can be wrong.

**Its limit:** this is not JustinGuitar with another instructor. Riff Quest does not supply the same filmed course or lesson sequence. Guitar Pro import also means files you bring yourself; it does not transfer a competitor's paid library. The combination is a manual workflow: learn elsewhere, practise a matching task here and record the result.

If paper is easier to keep beside the guitar, the [free practice journal](/guitar-practice-journal) is another way to make that workflow concrete. Write a specific goal and a next step. You can start doing that without committing to any new app.

## 4. Songsterr: Work on the Songs You Choose

Songsterr makes sense when your question is about a specific guitar part rather than the next lesson in a course. Its [official description](https://www.songsterr.com/about) explains the tab archive and player. Use it to inspect a passage, identify the rhythm and decide whether the arrangement is something you can work on now.

**Choose it if:** you have enough technique and tab-reading knowledge to learn from written material, and the song you want is not covered by your current lessons. Check the instrument, tuning and difficulty before trying to follow the whole track. Two useful bars can make a better practice task than an ambitious complete song.

**Its limit:** playback alone does not explain why a movement feels awkward. You may still need a lesson on alternate picking, muting or chord changes before the passage becomes manageable. Songsterr's [paid feature list](https://www.songsterr.com/plus) includes advanced controls such as looping and playback-speed adjustment, so evaluate the access you will actually use.

Our [Songsterr alternatives guide](/blog/songsterr-alternatives) compares song discovery with editing your own files and organising practice. Those are separate decisions. For example, a file editor may be excellent for a transcription you already own and still be the wrong replacement if your main need is finding arrangements.

## How to Switch Without Starting From Zero

Keep a short list of what you can already do and what you are currently trying to improve. Use concrete descriptions: changing between two chords without pausing, maintaining a rhythm for eight bars, or playing a short melody from memory. A course level or badge can be useful context, but a playable example tells a new teacher or routine much more.

Then choose one gap to work on for a week. You might keep the songs you know and change only the explanation of a difficult strumming pattern. Alternatively, keep the teacher and change how the repetitions are scheduled. This lets you observe the effect of the change without simultaneously replacing every part of your practice.

### A 25-Minute Comparison Session

Use this as an example, not a required duration. Pick a skill at your level and shorten any block when you need to.

| Minutes | Task | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Watch one explanation of the skill | Can you describe the next action without replaying the entire lesson? |
| 5-13 | Repeat a small exercise | Can you keep the task slow and comfortable enough to hear mistakes? |
| 13-22 | Put it into a short musical passage | Does the same movement work when another chord or phrase surrounds it? |
| 22-25 | Record a short attempt and note the next step | What will you repeat at the next session? |

Do not judge the course only by whether one session felt exciting. On the next day, try the same passage before reviewing the explanation. What you can recall, the questions you can now name and how readily you start practising are more useful observations than how many videos you completed.

If you need a longer structure, the [daily guitar practice plans](/daily-guitar-practice-plan) include several session lengths, and the [15-minute beginner practice routine](/blog/guitar-practice-routine-builder) is the shortest starting point. If the beginner material already feels comfortable, the [intermediate routine](/intermediate-guitar-practice-routine) keeps technique, rhythm, ear work and repertoire in the same week.

## FAQs

### Is JustinGuitar free, or do I need an alternative to avoid paying?

Many website lessons, including beginner material, are free. The app, Tabs and other paid products are separate offers. Check which product is asking for payment; you may be able to keep the website lessons and change only the accompanying tools.

### JustinGuitar vs Fender Play: which is better?

That depends on which explanation and practice workflow suit you. Compare the same beginner skill in both, check the relevant material and consider the cost of the specific product. This article does not claim a measured difference in learning outcomes between the courses.

### Is Andy Guitar completely free?

There are free starter videos, but the newer Beginner Course and membership are separate paid offers. Follow the current product page rather than assuming that a free video means the whole course is included.

### Can Riff Quest replace JustinGuitar's lessons?

It can replace or supplement parts of your practice routine, not the teacher's explanations. Use it for exercises, planning and recording sessions, and keep an appropriate lesson source for unfamiliar skills.

### Will my course progress transfer to another app?

No transfer is promised here. Keep your own notes about skills, exercises and song sections. A manually rebuilt routine does not mean the services share accounts, subscriptions or lesson history.

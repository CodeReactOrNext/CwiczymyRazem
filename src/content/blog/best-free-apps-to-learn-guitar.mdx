The guitar apps that stay free with no subscription are JustinGuitar's website course (its Beginner Grade Course 1 is described on its own page as completely free), Andy Guitar's free beginner lessons, Riff Quest's practice tools, and the free tab reading on Songsterr and Ultimate Guitar. A second group is free only up to a limit: Chordify allows two songs a day without an account, Simply Guitar gives away the first part of its Guitar Basics I course, GuitarTuna keeps its core tuner free, and Yousician offers only limited free access. A third group only looks free: the Yousician Premium+ trial lasts seven days and turns into a paid subscription unless it is cancelled, the Simply Guitar trial applies to the yearly plan, and Fender Play is a free trial followed by a subscription. The Gibson App had no free tier when last checked. One free lesson source, one free tab source and one practice log cost nothing and leave no trial to cancel.

**Short version: take JustinGuitar's free Grade 1 course for the lessons, read tabs on Songsterr or Ultimate Guitar for the songs, and use Riff Quest for the routine, the practice log and note feedback.** That stack has no card, no trial and no monthly charge.

Official pages for JustinGuitar, Simply Guitar, Chordify and Yousician were reviewed on **16 October 2026**. Details for the other apps come from earlier checks described at the end of this guide.

## What "Free" Means for Guitar Apps

Guitar apps use the word free for four different things, and only the first one means no cost for as long as you keep playing.

| Label | What it means | Apps in this guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Free for good** | The core learning or practice tools stay free, with no card and no time limit | JustinGuitar's website course, Andy Guitar, Riff Quest |
| **Free with limits** | Reading or basic use is free, while playback, extra lessons or daily use is capped or paid | Songsterr, Ultimate Guitar, Chordify, GuitarTuna, Simply Guitar, Yousician |
| **Free trial** | Full access for a fixed period, then a charge unless the trial is cancelled | Yousician Premium+, Simply Guitar yearly plan, Fender Play |
| **Paid from day one** | No free access at all | Gibson App |

A free trial is not a free app. Yousician's support pages state that starting the Premium+ trial requires a payment method, and the terms say a paid subscription begins automatically unless the trial is cancelled first. [Yousician trial conditions](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000336137-How-to-start-a-free-premium-trial)

Prices are left out of this guide on purpose. Several vendors, Yousician in particular, price their plans by country, and published figures for Fender Play, Simply Guitar and Coach Guitar disagree between sources, so any single number would mislead. The vendor's own page in your own currency is the reliable source.

### Riff Quest: Free Practice Tools With No Card

Riff Quest has no paywall. The exercise library, ready-made routines, Auto Plan, Free Timer, Manual Log, Pitch Detect and the practice history are free, and the project is supported by voluntary donations rather than subscriptions.

Pitch Detect listens through a microphone or an audio interface in supported exercises and checks whether the notes played match the tablature. The check covers pitch only, not tone, dynamics or the quality of a whole performance, and the [note detection](/wiki/note-detection) page explains the limits.

Riff Quest is not a video course and has no licensed play-along song catalogue. A free lesson source such as JustinGuitar supplies the teaching, and Riff Quest supplies the routine, the feedback on notes and the record of what was practised.

I log a JustinGuitar lesson in Manual Log the same way as a Riff Quest exercise, so a week of mixed practice ends up in one history instead of three apps.

Riff Quest runs in a web browser, so it works on a phone without an app-store install. There is no separate iOS or Android app, and an optional desktop app adds native audio input.

## Apps That Are Free With Limits

### Songsterr and Ultimate Guitar: Free to Read, Paid to Play Back

Songsterr lets anyone browse and read tabs for free. Songsterr Plus adds sync with the original audio, speed changes without pitch changes, looping, solo and mute per instrument, and downloads. [Songsterr Plus](https://www.songsterr.com/a/wa/plus)

Ultimate Guitar hosts community tabs and chord charts for essentially every popular song, and reading them is free. Ultimate Guitar Pro is the paid tier that unlocks playback and offline saving. [Ultimate Guitar Pro](https://www.ultimate-guitar.com/pro)

A free song-learning setup reads the tab on either site and plays it against the original recording and a [free browser metronome](/tools/metronome) with tempo control. The playback tools are the convenience being skipped, not the song.

### Chordify: Two Songs a Day Without an Account

Chordify works out the chords of a recording automatically. Its support centre says a visitor can play up to 2 songs per day on the website, and a free account raises that to 4. The iOS app counts differently, so the website figures are the ones to rely on. [How do I get my free songs?](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/articles/360020111898-How-do-I-get-my-free-songs)

The free version shows advertisements and leaves out features such as capo hints and file uploads, which belong to Chordify Premium. The daily figure can change, so it is worth confirming on the site. [Is Chordify free?](https://support.chordify.net/hc/en-us/articles/360001945038-Is-Chordify-free)

## Free Trials That Turn Into Subscriptions

Three apps in this guide lead with a trial instead of a free tier: Yousician Premium+, the Simply Guitar yearly plan and Fender Play. Each is a way to evaluate the product, not a free practice plan.

The Yousician Premium+ trial lasts seven days, requires a payment method and starts a paid subscription automatically unless it is cancelled before the trial ends. The Simply Guitar seven-day trial is available only on the yearly plan, and each account or Apple ID gets one trial.

The Gibson App had no free tier when last checked in June 2026, so a purchase comes before any trial of the lessons.

Four habits prevent a surprise charge from a trial:

- Check whether a payment method is required before the trial starts.
- Set a reminder for the day before the trial ends.
- Note which plan the trial is tied to, since the Simply Guitar trial covers only the yearly plan.
- Cancel the subscription in the app-store account settings, because deleting the app does not cancel it.

## FAQs

### Are there completely free apps to learn guitar?

Yes. JustinGuitar's Beginner Grade Course 1 on its website is described as completely free, Andy Guitar offers free beginner lessons with a free account, and Riff Quest's exercise library, routines and practice history are free with no card. Tab sites such as Songsterr and Ultimate Guitar are free to read but charge for playback tools.

### Is JustinGuitar really free?

The Beginner Grade Course 1 on the JustinGuitar website is described on its own page as completely free. The separately offered Lessons & Songs app and the premium products are not part of that free website course, so start on the website if spending nothing is the goal.

### Which free guitar app is best for a complete beginner?

JustinGuitar's free Beginner Grade Course 1 is the strongest free path for lessons, because the course runs from guitar basics through open chords and first songs in a fixed order. A practice log such as Riff Quest's Manual Log then records the sessions, since no free course tracks time spent playing.

### Are free-trial guitar apps free?

No. A free trial gives full access for a fixed period and then charges unless it is cancelled. The Yousician Premium+ trial lasts seven days and requires a payment method, and the Simply Guitar seven-day trial is available only on the yearly plan.

### Is Yousician free?

Partly. Yousician's terms allow access to parts of the service without paying, but the official documentation does not state a daily allowance, and reviewers describe roughly 15 minutes of play per day. The seven-day Premium+ trial is a paid subscription with a delayed start, not free access.

### Can a free app give feedback on what I play?

Yes, for notes. Riff Quest's Pitch Detect checks in the browser whether the notes played match an exercise, and the free Rocksmith alternatives such as StringTheory and Soundlane score whole songs from Guitar Pro files. None of the free options judges tone, dynamics or overall musicality.

### Do free guitar apps work on a phone?

Some do. Songsterr and Ultimate Guitar have iOS and Android apps, and Riff Quest runs in a phone browser without an app-store install. JustinGuitar's free course is a website, while Yousician and Simply Guitar offer mobile apps with limited free access.

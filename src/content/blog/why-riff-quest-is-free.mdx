Riff Quest is free because the people who use it pay for it voluntarily. Donations through Buy Me a Coffee cover all of the running costs, and in a good month there is a little left over for a game on Steam or a new set of strings. That is enough for me to drop subscriptions and paywalls completely, so every exercise, routine, song and ranking in Riff Quest is open to every player. I took the model from streamers and free-to-play games: the thing itself costs nothing, and the people who get something out of it can chip in if they want to. Riff Quest is built by one person, a programmer who plays guitar in a band, and the rest of this page is about who that is and what the app is for.

## Who Pays for Riff Quest

Players pay for Riff Quest, and only the ones who choose to. Donations come in through [Buy Me a Coffee](https://buymeacoffee.com/riffquest), and they cover the costs of running the app without any trouble. Thank you to everyone who has sent one.

Riff Quest has no subscription, no premium tier and no trial that turns into a charge. A new player does not enter a card number at any point, because there is nothing to charge for.

Streamers and free-to-play games work the same way, and that is the business model I chose, if a hobby project can be said to have one. Most people watch or play for free. A smaller group supports the thing because they like it and want it to continue.

Supporters get a say in what is built next: they vote on the public roadmap. Practising itself is the same for everyone.

## Who Builds Riff Quest

I am a programmer, and I play guitar in a band called Sublunar. Riff Quest is where those two halves of my week meet.

Riff Quest started about five years ago as a project I could show in job interviews. A practice tracker was a natural thing to build, because I was a guitarist who wanted one.

A few years later I came back to that old project and started improving it properly. Working on Riff Quest lets me combine programming and guitar in one place and build a practice app out of the two.

## Why I Use AI to Build It

I use AI tools to build Riff Quest, and I would rather say so than have anyone guess. An app of this size has exercises with tabs, a song library, rankings, a desktop version and a lot more behind them. One person cannot keep all of that running and improving without that help.

What surprised me is how much work is still left. I have years of experience building applications, and I have AI doing a share of the typing, and getting a feature to a quality I am happy with still takes far more time than I expected. The tools make the project possible for one person. They do not make it quick.

## What Riff Quest Is Meant to Be

Riff Quest is meant for serious practice, and I think that is missing from most guitar apps. Most of them are built around playing along to songs, around video lessons, or only around beginners. Fewer are built for the player who already knows what to work on and needs a place to do the work and see whether it is paying off.

Riff Quest is also built around its community. When I started playing, a friend and I were always trying to outdo each other, and that healthy rivalry kept both of us practising. The rankings, the seasons and the duels in Riff Quest are my attempt to give other players the same feeling.

The third thing I care about is not walking away. Plenty of apps get a few months of development and are then left as they are. I want to keep the quality of Riff Quest up, listen to what players tell me and keep improving it. Feedback mostly reaches me through the Riff Quest Discord server.
